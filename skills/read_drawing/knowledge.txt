Each sector has unique technical demands. Below are detailed explanations of how these industries typically apply rubber rollers, along with important technical insights such as optimal hardness ranges, temperature requirements, and more.


--------------------------------------------------------
2.1 PCB (Printed Circuit Boards)
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Laminating multi-layer boards, pressing dry film photoresist, solder mask application, cleaning lines.
- Roller Functions: Hot-press lamination rollers, nip rollers for dry film attachment, cleaning rollers for dust removal.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • High Temperature Resistance: Silicone-based compounds withstand 200–260°C.
  • Chemical Resistance: Must tolerate etchants, fluxes, and cleaning solutions.
  • Hardness Range: 50–90 Shore A for uniform pressure without board warpage.
  • Release Properties: Non-stick surfaces to prevent residue buildup during lamination.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.2 Optoelectronic Panels
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: TFT-LCD, STN, TN panel cleaning, light guide plate (LGP) preparation, polarizer film application.
- Roller Functions: Anti-static cleaning rollers, conductive dust-removal rollers, nip rollers for film attachment.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Surface Sensitivity: Panels can be easily scratched; thus, soft to medium hardness rollers with consistent surface finish are crucial.
  • Anti-Static Requirements: Reduces electrostatic discharge that attracts dust.
  • Surface Finish: Polished or specialized coatings to ensure gentle yet effective contact with panels.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Packaging Industry
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Lamination of plastic films, bag-making, carton sealing, printing on packaging materials.
- Roller Functions: Nip rollers, drive rollers, tension rollers, heat sealing rollers.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Grip vs. Release: Polyurethane for traction in feeding lines; silicone when release properties are needed.
  • Heat Resistance: Hot lamination requires materials like silicone or EPDM.
  • Durability: Abrasion-resistant compounds for high-speed or high-volume lines.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.4 Film Industry
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Coating, slitting, thermal lamination, metalizing.
- Roller Functions: Nip rollers to press and guide thin films, tension control rollers, cooling rollers.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Thickness Sensitivity: Films can be extremely thin, requiring precise roller alignment and controlled nip pressure.
  • Temperature Control: In lamination or coating, rollers may include internal cooling or heating systems.
  • Surface Finish: Extremely smooth rollers to prevent film scratching or unwanted texture.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.5 Electronics Industry (Non-PCB)
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Component assembly, chip packaging, battery manufacturing.
- Roller Functions: Transport rollers in pick-and-place lines, conductive/ESD rollers in sensitive assembly areas.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • ESD Protection: Anti-static or conductive rollers prevent static damage to electronic components.
  • Precision Handling: Rollers must run with low runout to avoid misalignment of micro-components.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.6 Printing Industry
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Offset printing, flexographic printing, gravure printing, embossing.
- Roller Functions: Ink transfer rollers, impression rollers, metering rollers.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Solvent Resistance: Rollers must endure inks and cleaning chemicals.
  • Hardness: Typically 30–80 Shore A, depending on ink transfer vs. impression.
  • Precision Ground Finishes: Consistent ink transfer and minimal dot gain.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.7 Steel & Metal Processing
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Coil handling, tension leveling, surface coating, galvanizing.
- Roller Functions: Tension rollers, bridle rollers, drive rollers in metal strip lines.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • High Load Tolerance: Polyurethane with high durometer (80–95 Shore A or even Shore D) can handle heavy loads.
  • Abrasion Resistance: Metal edges can be sharp, necessitating tough roller covers.
  • Dimensional Stability: Prevent deflection under heavy tension.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.8 Woodworking Machinery
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Planing, sanding, laminating veneer, edge-banding.
- Roller Functions: Infeed/outfeed rollers, pressure rollers, glue spreading rollers.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Wear Resistance: Must tolerate wood dust and resin.
  • Grip: Polyurethane or high-grip NBR for consistent material feeding.
  • Chemical Compatibility: Some adhesives or finishes may require solvent-resistant compounds.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.9 Textiles
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Dyeing, printing, finishing, calendering, coating, and lamination.
- Roller Functions: Squeeze rollers, drying rollers, embossing rollers.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Chemical Resistance: Dye baths, bleaching agents, finishing chemicals.
  • Temperature Tolerance: Hot processes may demand EPDM or silicone.
  • Soft Finish: Lower hardness rollers (30–60 Shore A) can conform to delicate fabrics.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.10 Conveyors
--------------------------------------------------------
- Common Processes: Material handling in warehouses, distribution centers, manufacturing lines.
- Roller Functions: Idler rollers, drive rollers, tension rollers.
- Key Technical Knowledge:
  • Shock Absorption: Needed for heavy or impact loading.
  • Traction: Grooved or patterned polyurethane or NBR surfaces.
  • Environmental Factors: Heat, cold, moisture, or chemical exposure.

--------------------------------------------------------
2.11 Other Industries
--------------------------------------------------------
- Examples: Food processing (FDA-compliant rollers), automotive parts manufacturing, agricultural machinery.
- Customized Compounds: Food-grade silicone, anti-bacterial coatings, or other specialized materials.
- Key Technical Knowledge: Must meet regulatory requirements and handle specific chemical or temperature challenges.

3. PRODUCT & MATERIAL DETAILS
========================================================

--------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Material Options
--------------------------------------------------------
1) NR (Natural Rubber): Excellent elasticity, moderate wear resistance.
2) SBR (Styrene-Butadiene Rubber): Cost-effective, decent abrasion properties.
3) NBR (Nitrile Rubber): Superior oil/fuel resistance, common for printing.
4) CR (Neoprene): Good weather/ozone resistance, moderate oil resistance.
5) FKM/Viton: Exceptional chemical and heat resistance.
6) CSM (Hypalon): Remarkable resistance to ozone, chemicals, and high temperatures.
7) Silicone: High-temperature tolerance, excellent release (non-stick) properties.
8) EPDM: Great for steam, heat, and outdoor exposure; common in textiles and paper.
9) Polyurethane (PU): High abrasion resistance, load-bearing capacity, and cut resistance.

--------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Hardness (Durometer)
--------------------------------------------------------
- Shore A Range (20–95): Most common for rubber.
- Shore D: Used for extremely high-load or high-wear scenarios.
- Selection Factors:
  • Load & Pressure: Higher durometer (70–95 Shore A) for heavy loads.
  • Conforming to Surfaces: Lower durometer (30–60 Shore A) for delicate materials or better grip.
  • Process Speed: High-speed lines often need harder rollers to maintain shape.

--------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Surface Finishes
--------------------------------------------------------
- Smooth/Polished: Uniform coating or ink transfer.
- Grooved/Patterned: Enhanced traction, fluid drainage, or specialized embossing.
- Conductive/Antistatic: Essential for electronics to reduce static issues.
- Textured/Lagging: Often used in conveyor rollers for improved grip.
